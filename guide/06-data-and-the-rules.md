# 6. Data, privacy and the rules: what to settle before the first session

*What an AI agent on your computer can reach, what the rules in force in October 2026 say, and the few decisions to
take before you put anyone else's material into a knowledge base. About ten minutes to read. The quotations are from
the sources as they read in October 2026; the addresses are in the list at the end.*

## Four facts about the tool, in Anthropic's own words

Anthropic is the company that makes Claude and Claude Code. Its documentation states four facts that everything else
in this guide follows from.

1. **It works as you.** Claude Code reads and writes files with your own permissions. Anything you can open, it can
   open: a OneDrive folder that is copied to Microsoft's cloud, a network drive (a shared DTU folder that appears as a
   drive letter such as `M:` in File Explorer), a file with passwords in it.
2. **What it reads leaves the computer.** "Claude Code sends data over the network. This data includes all user
   prompts and model outputs" [1]. A prompt is what you type or dictate; the files the agent opens become part of the
   prompt, so they are sent to Anthropic's servers to be processed.
3. **The terms depend on your plan.** A personal Pro, Max or Ultra plan comes with Anthropic's consumer terms [2], its
   contract for private customers, which are written for a person acting outside their profession and say
   "Non-commercial use only". The commercial terms [3] (the Team and Enterprise plans, which an organisation buys, and
   the paid programming interface) say that Anthropic "may not train models on Customer Content", and come with a
   data processing agreement: the contract that the GDPR (the General Data Protection Regulation of the European
   Union) requires when another company processes personal data on your behalf.
4. **Written rules are advice, not a lock.** The agent "treats them as context, not enforced configuration. To block
   an action regardless of what Claude decides, use a PreToolUse hook instead" [4]. A hook is a small script that
   Claude Code runs before each action of the agent (PreToolUse is Anthropic's name for that moment), and which can
   refuse the action. That is why the template protects the originals in `raw/` with a hook, the guard hook, and not
   only with a sentence in its rules file.

**How to find out which terms apply to you.** Open claude.ai, then Settings: the name of your plan is shown there. Pro,
Max and Ultra bought by you are personal plans under the consumer terms. A Team or Enterprise plan is bought by an
organisation for its staff; ask DTU's IT support whether DTU offers one.

## The training setting, product by product

On a personal plan you decide whether your conversations may be used to train the provider's future models, which
means using them to teach the next version of the AI. Check the setting in each product before the first session, and
read it as it is on the day rather than trusting this list (read on 30 October 2026 from the makers' pages [5]).

| Product | Where the setting is | What "off" means |
|---|---|---|
| Claude, and Claude Code on the same login | claude.ai, Settings, Privacy, "Help improve Claude" | your conversations are not used for future training, and are kept 30 days instead of up to 5 years; what earlier conversations gave to models already trained stays in them |
| Wispr Flow | Settings, Data and Privacy, "Improve the model for everyone" | no training on your dictations; the audio is still turned into text on the maker's computers in the United States |
| Meetily | nothing to set: it transcribes on your computer | a summary made in the cloud, if you choose one, sends the transcript |
| ChatGPT | Settings, Data controls, "Improve the model for everyone" | no training; your earlier conversations stay in your list |
| Gemini | "Keep Activity" | no training, but chats are still kept for 72 hours, and a sample read by people for up to three years |

## What the rules say

1. **You stay the author, and you check everything the agent writes.** The European Commission's "living guidelines"
   on generative AI in research (guidelines it updates as the field changes; version of 15 October 2026) [6]:
   researchers "are accountable for the integrity of the content they produce generated by or with the support of AI
   tools", and "the use of AI agents may never be considered a delegation of control or responsibility". None of the
   publishers' policies or codes of conduct read for the seminar accepts an AI as an author.
2. **Declare substantial use where a recipient asks for it**, in the form it asks. Substantial use means using AI as a
   method, in a literature review, in interpreting results, or in drafting text. The Danish Code of Conduct for
   Research Integrity (January 2026) [7] requires AI use in publication to be declared; Elsevier, Springer Nature,
   Taylor and Francis and IEEE ask for a statement in the manuscript; Horizon Europe and the Danish research funds ask
   for one in the application. A knowledge base under version control keeps a record of what the agent did, which
   makes such a statement easy to write and to support.
3. **Personal and confidential material only in a tool with an agreement that covers it.** DTU's guidelines on AI [8]:
   "If you are working with confidential material, you should stick to AI tools where we have a data processing
   agreement with the provider." Personal data means any information about an identifiable person, including what
   they said in a recorded meeting. DTU lists Claude and Claude Code among "other AI tools", "not assessed by AIT"
   (DTU's AI and IT department), which means that DTU has not reviewed them, not that they are forbidden; they may be
   used for non-critical data [9]. Whether research under a personal plan fits the consumer terms is a question for
   DTU's legal office. Until it is answered, keep other people's personal and confidential material out of what the
   agent reads.
4. **Upload only what you may upload**: your own work; material published under an open licence (such as Creative
   Commons) or in the public domain; and material covered by the copyright exception for text and data mining in
   research (the rule in EU and Danish law that lets researchers analyse works they have lawful access to). Not a
   technical standard bought under licence (a DS or EN standard), a publisher's article under DTU's subscription
   unless its licence allows it, or a colleague's teaching material without their agreement.
5. **Tell the people whose words you record**, and say how you protect them. Teams tells everyone; a local recorder
   such as Meetily tells no one, so you say it at the start of the meeting.

## The decisions to take on day one

- **Which terms your account is under** (above), and therefore what may go into the knowledge base. If in doubt, ask
  DTU before you deposit anyone else's words.
- **One working folder, on a computer that shows the agent nothing else of DTU's.** Keep the projects in
  `C:\dev\projects`, not in OneDrive, because OneDrive copies everything to Microsoft's cloud and the agent can read it
  all. Disconnect network drives while you work with the agent, because a mapped drive lets the agent reach shared DTU
  files that are not yours to send.
- **Who is never named** in the knowledge base. The project's rules file, `AGENTS.md`, has a rule for it (rule 7,
  "People"), which the set-up fills in with you. The script that turns originals into text (`tools/extract.py`)
  replaces the speaker names of Teams transcripts with "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2" unless it is run with the option
  `--keep-speakers`.
- **The training settings off**, in every product you use.

One more thing learned the hard way: **expect the agent's first draft of the rules to be more cautious than your case
needs.** Its own default is to withhold names and data "to be safe": for example, to refuse to name the members of
your own project group in your own notes. Decide what the knowledge base may hold, with the reason, and write it into
`AGENTS.md`. The caution belongs to what leaves the project (a paper, a report, a slide), not to what the knowledge
base holds for you.

## Terms used

- **Prompt**: what you type or dictate to the agent, together with the files it opens for the task.
- **Consumer terms**: the provider's contract for private customers, which applies to a personal plan.
- **Data processing agreement**: the contract the GDPR requires when another company processes personal data for you.
- **Personal data**: any information about an identifiable person, including their words in a recording.
- **Hook**: a small script that Claude Code runs before each action of the agent, and which can refuse the action.
- **Training**: a provider's use of conversations to teach the next version of its AI.
- **Network drive**: a shared folder on DTU's network that appears as a drive letter in File Explorer.
