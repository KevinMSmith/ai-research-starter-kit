# Plain: re-explain in plain teaching English

The reader found something hard to follow. Re-explain it for an intelligent reader who is new to the field. Do not
answer a different question, and do not add new work.

## With no argument: the previous answer

1. **Re-explain, do not re-answer.** Take your previous message and explain the same content again. Keep every fact,
   number, file name and recommendation exactly as it was.
2. **Open with the situation**: one or two sentences that say what was asked and where things stand.
3. **Define every term** a newcomer would not know, in plain words, where it first appears. Write abbreviations in
   full with the abbreviation in brackets.
4. **Turn every choppy fragment into a complete statement with its background**: a statement that uses a term from
   outside the reader's field, or that is not specific, gets a clause of background or reason. Labels may stay; expand
   one with a few words where the reader might not know what it refers to.
5. **Use full sentences**, one idea each, fifteen to twenty words on average. No chains joined by semicolons, no arrows
   or symbols for words, no idiom, no invented labels; use the standard name of each thing. Aim for the middle:
   complete, not padded.
6. **End** with what the reader should decide or do next, and a short "Terms used" list if more than three terms were
   defined.

Simpler does not mean shorter: the re-explanation is usually longer than the original, but not padded.

## With a file: rewrite a document

1. Rewrite the document in place under the same rules. Keep its structure, its headings, every fact, every quotation
   (quotations are never reworded), every number and every link. Do not touch code blocks or tables of data.
2. Report in two or three sentences the changes that mattered most.

Never rewrite an original in a folder the project's rules file declares unchangeable (such as `raw/`); write the plain
version beside it instead.
